# SpidyVR update — 6 September 2026

## Five requested changes

1. **Smooth turning** — replaced right-stick snap/latch logic with continuous analog yaw, integrated using frame delta. An 18% dead zone rejects drift, quadratic response gives finer low-speed control, and full deflection reaches 120 degrees/second. Input is applied before converting headset and controller poses to world space. Turning does not rotate existing world momentum or clear the hand-stroke history. Left-stick locomotion and right-stick vertical behavior are preserved.
2. **Visible web travel** — a separate reusable flight state per hand holds the existing target point. On firing, a short synthesized spatial “thwip” plays from that hand, its emitter flashes, and a bright web tip travels toward the building with a continuous strand back to the moving emitter. Travel is 75–220 ms, depending on range. A short surface-aligned impact ring and haptic mark contact. Only then is the unchanged physics attachment called. Release, reset, pause, lost tracking, and hidden sessions cancel pending flights. Flight arrival checks for a newly obstructing building. Existing hand offsets and physics are retained after contact; the rendered web meets the visible shooter.
3. **Roof flicker root cause** — the old full-width `roof-rim` box had its top exactly at the building mesh's own top plane. Both competed for the depth buffer. Removed that redundant roof slab and replaced its trim with four narrow exterior ledges below the deck elevation. The original building top is now the only roof deck. No global shadow/depth/material feature was disabled. A transformed-triangle regression check rejects upward-facing decorative surfaces coplanar with a roof interior. Texture mipmapping and modest anisotropy remain enabled.
4. **Panel hidden during gameplay** — immersive panel visibility is strictly `immersive && paused`. Startup, timer expiry, changing pull strength, speed, jump charge, swinging, and reset cannot show it. Y reveals it on pause and immediately hides it on resume. DOM status overlays are also hidden in immersive play. Targeting markers remain, as part of aiming.
5. **Living city** — retains exactly all 105 original building AABBs, including rooftop heights. Adds brick/stone/glass facade styles, varied windows and shutters, cornices and pilasters, entrances, canopies, shared-atlas signs, HVAC fan details, water tanks, rooftop access structures, solar panels, lamps, traffic signals, benches, bins, and more crosswalks. Sidewalks are slightly wider so people fit outside the preserved building walls. Adds moving cars, walking people with animated limbs, flapping birds in street corridors, one occasional distant aircraft, a gradient sky, and subtle GPU leaf motion.

## Quest-oriented optimization

- Architectural details are instanced with shared low-poly geometry; eight sign variants share one 1024×256 atlas.
- Four shared facade textures use mipmaps and 4× anisotropy. No high-resolution per-building texture sets.
- Traffic has a fixed pool of 56 vehicles; detailed models switch to two-box silhouettes beyond roughly 80 m and are culled at 330 m.
- There are 96 pedestrian routes; detailed animated bodies are used within roughly 48 m, simplified silhouettes farther away, and no pedestrians beyond 185 m in **3D** distance. Routes stay outside the existing buildings.
- Eighteen pooled birds are culled beyond 270 m. Their wing motion and pedestrian gait use inexpensive vertex animation. Tree leaves also sway in the vertex shader.
- Dynamic visible instance transforms update each render frame so cars do not visibly step at a lower update frequency. No per-frame mesh/geometry creation. Shared pools are bounded.
- One low-poly aircraft crosses in the distance for 36 seconds in a 105-second cycle. No aircraft AI or collision simulation.
- Webs reuse two flight states, two web meshes, two tips, two impact meshes, one synthesized firing sample, and two spatial panners.
- No shadow maps, real-time reflections, bloom pipeline, SSAO, or expensive postprocessing was added.

At the sampled street/roof/air positions, the **city alone** uses 73–76 draw calls and approximately 92,200–94,600 triangles per eye, before hands/webs/optional comfort rendering and before renderer frustum rejection. These are deterministic geometry budgets, not a Quest GPU benchmark. The test host simulates ten seconds of physics plus animated city updates in well under one second; this is likewise not a headset FPS measurement.

## Validation

The suite runs the actual game module against synthetic Quest-shaped sessions, controller poses/buttons/sticks, and a non-rendering renderer substitute. It exercises the actual input functions and animation loop, not a separate replacement movement implementation.

Coverage includes:

- Right-stick turning while grounded, walking, airborne, and web attached; continuous samples, drift rejection, and matching integrated rotation at 72/90/120 Hz.
- Both web shooters, distinct spatial audio panner positions at firing time, visible pending flights, no early attachment, impact transition, pulling, release, immediate re-fire, and dual webs.
- Pause/resume, reset, session exit/re-entry, and hidden-session cancellation; panel hidden during active traversal.
- Original gravity, slack rope, release momentum, downward/backward/dual pulling, collision, jump height, and long dual-rope simulation regressions.
- Exact preservation of all 105 building bounds, absence of the redundant roof slab, and transformed upward-face checks against every roof interior.
- Animated populations at 2, 32, 80, 150, and 240 m height; LOD and culling; pedestrian routes outside buildings; paused population behavior; high-speed dense-city physics/environment workload.

**Limitations:** The cloud browser security policy rejected the preview URL, so rendered visual QA and real WebGL shader execution could not be completed in that browser. No physical Quest 3 was available. Close/distant roof appearance during rapid headset movement, actual stereo depth behavior, sound quality, subjective turn feel, and sustained Quest frame rate still need hardware verification. The structural z-fighting cause is fixed, but no claim of a visually observed flicker-free headset result or locked headset frame rate is made.
